How to choose a trading community in the UK: 10 red flags

Paid trading and investing groups range from genuinely useful to outright scams. Here’s how to tell the difference before you pay.

Paid trading and investing groups are everywhere: on WhatsApp, Telegram, Discord and Instagram. Some are genuinely useful. Plenty are built to sell you a dream, a broker account or a course, and you only find out after you’ve paid. Here are the red flags to check before you join any of them, and what good looks like instead.

10 red flags to watch for

1. Guaranteed returns or eye-popping win rates

“90%+ accuracy”, “£1,000 a day from your phone”, “never lose again”. Nobody can promise returns in markets, and a win rate means nothing without the size of the losses. If a group leads with numbers like these, it’s selling a dream, not an education.

2. A wall of profit screenshots

Screenshots are easy to cherry-pick and impossible to check. A group worth joining shows its process: why it took a trade, where it would be wrong, and what happened, losses included.

3. Copy-and-paste calls with no reasoning

“Buy gold now, stop here, target there” teaches you nothing, and leaves you stuck the day the calls stop. Look for ideas that come with the thinking behind them, so you learn to make your own decisions.

4. No losses, ever

Every trader and investor loses on some ideas. A track record with no losing trades is either hidden or invented. Ask to see the bad calls and how they were handled.

5. Undisclosed broker links

Some groups earn commission when you open an account with a particular broker, and push you towards it without saying so. Referral links aren’t wrong in themselves, but they should be disclosed clearly and never be a condition of joining.

6. “Send us your money and we’ll trade it for you”

Managing other people’s money is a regulated activity in the UK. Never hand over your login details or send funds to a group or an individual who isn’t authorised to do it. Check the FCA Register first.

7. Leverage pushed on beginners

CFDs, spread bets and other leveraged products magnify losses as well as gains, and most retail traders lose money with them. A responsible group explains risk and position sizing before it ever talks about leverage.

8. Pressure and FOMO

“Only 3 spots left”, countdown timers and “price doubles tonight” are sales tactics. A good community will still be there next week, and will let you try it first.

9. No real business behind it

Look for a registered company you can find on Companies House, a named founder and a way to contact them. Anonymous groups that only exist in a chat app leave you with nowhere to go if something goes wrong.

10. Hard to leave

Hidden renewal terms, no cancel button and “email us 30 days in advance” are warning signs. You should be able to see the price, the billing cycle and how to cancel before you pay.

What good looks like

  • It explains why, not just what: the reasoning, the risk and what would change its mind.
  • It shows its track record honestly, losses included.
  • It teaches risk management and position sizing before anything else.
  • It discloses any commission it earns, and never makes a broker a condition of joining.
  • There’s a real company and a named person behind it.
  • Pricing and cancellation are clear, ideally with a free trial.
  • It makes clear it provides education, not personal financial advice.

Your quick checklist before paying

  • Search the group’s name with “reviews” and “scam”, and read the low-star reviews too.
  • Look the company up on Companies House.
  • If anyone offers to manage your money or give personal advice, check them on the FCA Register and the FCA’s ScamSmart warning list.
  • Read the subscription terms: price, billing cycle and how to cancel.
  • Start with a free option (a newsletter, a free channel or a trial) before committing.
